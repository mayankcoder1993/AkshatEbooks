# Wellbeing and Self-Help Book Profile

Use for self-reflection, habits, emotional wellbeing and personal-growth titles. Do not use it to imitate clinical treatment.

## Required brief fields

Reader age, intended outcome, evidence level, author qualifications, topics excluded, potential triggers, professional-review plan and region-specific support resources.

## Evidence and claims

Prefer recognized public-health bodies, clinical guidelines, systematic reviews and qualified expert sources. Distinguish evidence-based practices from tradition, personal reflection and author opinion. Never diagnose the reader or promise healing, cure, transformation or a specific result.

## Chapter design

- Explain the purpose and limit of each practice.
- Offer small, optional exercises with permission to pause or skip.
- Use neutral reflection prompts rather than forced disclosure.
- Include realistic examples without presenting composites as real people.
- Avoid shame, blame and the suggestion that illness or trauma results from insufficient effort.
- Provide grounding, pacing and next-step guidance where emotionally intense topics appear.

## Safety boundary

State clearly that the book is educational and not a substitute for medical or mental-health care. Identify situations that need qualified professional help. For self-harm, abuse, psychosis, severe distress or immediate danger, direct readers to local emergency/crisis resources and trusted human support; do not attempt to provide crisis counselling through book exercises.

## Sensitive information

Do not publish identifiable personal stories without documented permission. Review trauma, child safety, eating disorders, substance use and medical claims with appropriately qualified professionals before publication.
