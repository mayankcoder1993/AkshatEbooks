## PART A: SUBJECT INVARIANTS

### A1: What Must Be Taught

A multidisciplinary hybrid module handles complex real-world topics
that do not fit neatly into a single discipline, requiring fluid transitions
between law, economics, technology, public policy, and history.
Exemplar topic: The Goods and Services Tax (GST) Architecture.

FOUNDATIONAL LAYER:
  The convergence premise: why single-discipline analysis fails to
    explain the system (e.g. GST cannot be understood purely as tax law
    without economic supply chains, constitutional amendments, and IT infrastructure).
  Historical and policy origin: the economic friction or societal crisis
    that compelled multi-system reform.
  Constitutional and legal authority: the legislative acts, constitutional
    amendments, and statutory definitions empowering the system.
  Operational architecture: the digital network, software protocols,
    and administrative pipelines executing the policy.

PRACTICAL LAYER:
  Multi-perspective analysis: examining a single transaction or event
    through three distinct lenses (Legal, Economic, Technological).
  Cross-system data flow: how legal definitions translate into software
    validation rules and economic reporting.
  Compliance and reconciliation workflows: matching real-world business
    operations with regulatory statutory filings and digital ledgers.
  Dispute and friction zones: where legal ambiguity clashes with software
    rigidity or economic realities.

PROFESSIONAL / GOVERNANCE LAYER:
  Institutional federalism: cooperative governance bodies (e.g. GST Council),
    voting mechanisms, consensus building, and federal dispute resolution.
  Systemic audits and fraud detection: network analysis, circular trading
    detection, algorithmic risk profiling, and statutory investigations.
  Reform lifecycle and continuous evolution: amendment cascades, notifications,
    circulars, and technological system updates.
  Macro-economic and societal assessment: revenue buoyancy, inflation impacts,
    formalization of the unorganized economy, and compliance burdens on small enterprises.

### A2: Proof System

Dual / Triple Validation Standards:
  Legal claims must cite specific Constitutional Articles, Statutory Sections,
    Rules, and Notifications.
  Economic assertions must provide mathematical proofs, tax incidence models,
    and empirical revenue data from government reports.
  Technological mechanisms must specify data schemas (JSON/XML), API contracts,
    portal validation routines, and hash signatures.
  All three dimensions must be reconciled in a unified proof ledger.

## PART B: CREATIVE VEHICLE PALETTE

VEHICLE 1: THE NATIONAL TAX COUNCIL ESCAPE ROOM
World: A high-level task force comprising a constitutional lawyer, a senior
  economist, a state revenue commissioner, and a cloud architect must draft
  and operationalize a nation-wide unified indirect tax framework under
  a 90-day legislative deadline.

VEHICLE 2: THE CROSS-BORDER SUPPLY CHAIN ODYSSEY
World: A logistics and manufacturing conglomerate moves goods across four
  Indian states, navigating e-way bills, interstate IGST calculations,
  warehouse consolidation, and digital toll integrations in real time.

VEHICLE 3: THE FINTECH COMPLIANCE TRIBUNAL
World: An algorithmic invoice-factoring platform discovers a massive
  circular-trading syndicate exploiting tax credit timing differences;
  financial analysts, data engineers, and revenue enforcement sleuths
  collaborate to trace the graph and preserve the digital trail.
