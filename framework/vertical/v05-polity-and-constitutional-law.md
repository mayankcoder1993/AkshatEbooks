## PART A: SUBJECT INVARIANTS

### A1: What Must Be Taught

FOUNDATIONAL LAYER:
  Constitutional framework: Preamble, Fundamental Rights,
    Directive Principles, and the Basic Structure doctrine.
  Separation of powers: the constitutional balance between
    the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary.
  Federalism: division of powers between Union and States,
    Union List, State List, and Concurrent List.
  Constitutional remedies: Articles 32 and 226, writs of
    Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Quo Warranto,
    and Certiorari.

PRACTICAL LAYER:
  Statutory interpretation: plain meaning, purposive construction,
    harmonious construction, and the golden rule.
  Case law analysis: ratio decidendi vs obiter dicta, binding
    precedent, and distinguishing cases.
  Administrative discretion: judicial review of executive action,
    natural justice principles (audi alteram partem, rule against bias).
  Legislative procedure: how a bill becomes law, committee stages,
    money bills vs ordinary bills, ordinance making power.

PROFESSIONAL / EXAM MASTERY LAYER:
  Landmark precedents: evolution of constitutional jurisprudence
    from Shankari Prasad to Kesavananda Bharati to Puttaswamy.
  Constitutional amendment process: Article 368 mechanisms,
    special majorities, and ratification by states.
  Emergency provisions: Articles 352, 356, and 360, safeguards,
    and historical applications.
  Mains answer architecture: balanced constitutional analysis,
    framing petitioner arguments, state defenses, and judicial reasoning.

### A2: Proof System

ALL legal propositions must cite authoritative legal sources:
  Exact Article or Section number of the Constitution or Act.
  Full case citation: Case Name, Year, Volume, Reporter, Page.
  Accurate quotation of key judicial holdings.
  No paraphrased legal rule presented as statutory text.

PYQ Integration:
  Every major constitutional principle must link directly to
    at least one verified UPSC or Judicial Services exam question.
  Marking rubric must mirror official judicial / UPSC evaluation criteria.

### A3: Interactive Element Types

Courtroom Drama Workbench (court-bench.svg.js):
  Shows the bench of judges, petitioner counsel, respondent counsel.
  Visualizes arguments, legal provisions invoked, and the bench's questions.

Constitutional Tree / Flowchart:
  Hierarchical visual breakdown of constitutional articles,
  exceptions, and proviso clauses.

Ratio Decidendi Card:
  Standardized 4-part visual card for case law:
    1. Facts of the Dispute
    2. Constitutional Question Framed
    3. Ratio Decidendi (Binding Rule)
    4. Practical Impact and Subsequent Applications

Writs Matrix:
  Side-by-side comparative table showing the 5 writs:
  Target authority, legal ground, standard remedy, and landmark case.

## PART B: CREATIVE VEHICLE PALETTE

VEHICLE 1: THE MOOT COURT BENCH
World: A high-stakes national moot court competition where law students
  argue a complex constitutional challenge involving fundamental rights
  and emerging technology before retired Supreme Court judges.

VEHICLE 2: THE DISTRICT MAGISTRATE'S DILEMMA
World: A newly posted IAS officer faces an urgent administrative crisis
  involving public order, minority rights, and executive overreach,
  navigating constitutional constraints under intense political pressure.
