# Audience Profile: Children (8 to 12 Years)

Profile ID: ap01
Target Age: 8 to 12 Years
Cognitive Tier: Concrete Operational to Early Formal Operational
Reading Level: Primary / Middle School

---

## 1. Demographic and Psychological Profile

Target Reader:
  Primary and middle school students learning foundational science,
  mathematics, logic, and social awareness.

Attention Span:
  10 to 15 minutes per session.
  Easily overwhelmed by long text blocks or multi-clause sentences.
  Requires frequent visual resets and physical interaction cues.

Core Motivation:
  Innate curiosity, playfulness, excitement about relatable story characters,
  feeling proud of solving small challenges, and collecting visual badges.

Frustration Triggers:
  Walls of abstract definitions without relatable physical toys or objects.
  Feeling judged, tested, or talked down to by an authoritarian adult voice.
  Dense multi-step logic without intermediate feedback or visual payoff.

---

## 2. Formatting and Calibration Rules

Tone and Voice:
  Playful, energetic, highly encouraging, and empathetic.
  Mistakes are treated as fun experiments and discovery moments, never failures.
  The mentor acts as a friendly older sibling or guide on an adventure.

Vocabulary Constraints:
  Simple, common words.
  Maximum two-syllable words for explanations where possible.
  Technical or domain terms must have immediate playful nicknames
  (for example, calling an API an "order ticket" or a router a "traffic light").

Sentence Length Limits:
  Maximum 12 words per sentence in dialogue.
  Maximum 15 words per sentence in narrative exposition boxes.
  Zero passive voice structures.

Prose Budget per Scene:
  10 to 25 words maximum per panel.
  The narrative action and pedagogical transformation must occur visually.

Visual Density and Panel Layout:
  4 to 6 panels per page.
  High visual motion, large facial expressions, and dynamic sound effect callouts.
  Bright primary and warm Indian folk art palettes (ochre, terracotta, turmeric).

Gameplay and Interactivity:
  High interactivity.
  Spot the difference puzzles, maze paths, matching games, and sticker badges.
  Predictive questions with 2 big illustrated choice buttons.

---

## 3. Chapter Special Rules

Opening Hook:
  Must open with an everyday sensory puzzle or curious physical mystery
  (for example, how a toy car moves, why a light switch clicks, or a missing samosa).

Closing Rule:
  Every chapter must conclude with a 5-minute hands-on physical activity
  using common household items (such as paper, coins, glasses of water, or spoons).
  Must include an unlockable illustrated Adventure Badge for the reader's collection.
