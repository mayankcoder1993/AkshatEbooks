# Audience Profile: Seniors (60+ Years)

Profile ID: ap06
Target Age: 60+ Years
Cognitive Tier: Reflective Lifelong Learning / Adaptive Digital Navigation
Reading Level: Senior Citizen / Accessible Digital Literacy

---

## 1. Demographic and Psychological Profile

Target Reader:
  Retired professionals, elders, and senior citizens learning to navigate modern digital services,
  smartphone banking, cyber safety, healthcare portals, government pensions, and digital governance.

Attention Span:
  20 to 30 minutes in a calm, distraction-free environment.
  May experience visual fatigue, reduced fine motor precision, or hesitation with fast touchscreens.

Core Motivation:
  Independence from relying on adult children for basic transactions, digital safety from fraudsters,
  staying connected with grandchildren and family, and understanding modern societal changes.

Frustration Triggers:
  Tiny fonts, low contrast text, rapidly flashing animations, unexplained technical icons,
  and fear of pressing the wrong button and losing money or breaking the device.

---

## 2. Formatting and Calibration Rules

Tone and Voice:
  Deeply respectful, patient, dignified, gentle, and encouraging.
  Honors their life wisdom while guiding them comfortably through modern digital tools.
  Reassures them that digital safety is attainable and mistakes can be safely undone.

Vocabulary Constraints:
  Clear, elegant, and straightforward language.
  Abstract digital metaphors are replaced with physical equivalents
  (for example, cloud storage as a digital safe deposit box, browser cache as temporary memo pad).

Sentence Length Limits:
  Maximum 14 words per sentence in dialogue.
  Maximum 16 words per sentence in narrative exposition boxes.

Prose Budget per Scene:
  15 to 30 words per panel.
  Text must always use large typography, high contrast, and generous line spacing.

Visual Density and Panel Layout:
  2 to 3 panels per page.
  Large, uncluttered panels with high contrast borders and clear finger-tap target indicators.
  Phone screens depicted with oversized buttons and clearly circled action steps.

Gameplay and Interactivity:
  Safe practice simulations.
  Step-by-step visual drills where readers trace safe steps on a mock screen with zero risk.
  Clear green checkmarks for safe actions and prominent red warning triangles for potential scams.

---

## 3. Chapter Special Rules

Opening Hook:
  Must open with an empathetic everyday scenario
  (for example, receiving a video call from grandchildren, renewing a digital life certificate,
  or receiving an urgent text message requesting banking details).

Closing Rule:
  Every chapter must conclude with a "Peace of Mind Safety Card"
  that can be printed or placed next to the phone or computer,
  summarizing what to do and what NEVER to do.
