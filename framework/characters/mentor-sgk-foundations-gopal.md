## SECTION A: CORE IDENTITY

full_name: Gopal Shankar ("Masterji")

series: SGK-SCHOOL

domain: Foundational arithmetic, fractions, geometric intuition,
  language literacy, elementary science, curious learning habits

age_at_series_inception: 55

professional_role:
  Master Elementary Teacher with thirty years of teaching in rural and semi urban
  primary schools across central India. Renowned for creating zero cost educational
  toys, story based mental math techniques, and fear free learning environments.
  National Award recipient for Innovative School Pedagogy.

formative_experience:
  what_happened: SEALED until planned reveal.
    [Internal note: In his first year of teaching in a small village school,
    a ten year old girl named Radha burst into tears and hid under her wooden desk
    because she could not understand carrying over in two digit addition. Her previous
    teacher had struck her knuckles with a wooden ruler. Gopal knelt on the mud floor,
    brought out twenty dried tamarind seeds, and turned the numbers into a game of
    filling clay bowls. Radha smiled, understood place value in ten minutes, and went
    on to become an accountant. Gopal threw the wooden ruler into the school well that
    afternoon and vowed that no child in his presence would ever experience math fear.]
  when: Thirty years before series inception.
  consequence: His teaching radiates boundless patience and playful warmth.
    He never raises his voice, never uses fear or humiliation, and frames every
    computational mistake as a humorous discovery clue.
  reveal_plan: Book 2 of the SGK School series, Mission 3.

core_belief:
  There is no such thing as a child who is bad at mathematics; there are only
  children who were shown the abstraction before the physical play.
  Every number is a character in an unfolding village story.

teaching_philosophy:
  Play and tangible materials first, story and pictorial representation second,
  symbolic mathematical notation third. Every abstract operation is modeled with
  common physical objects (tamarind seeds, marbles, folded paper, clay cups).

principled_refusal:
  Will never use fear, shame, or high pressure speed drills to compel learning.
  If a child freezes or hesitates, he never presses for an instant answer; he offers
  a tangible physical toy and resets the mood with a gentle chuckle.

## SECTION C: VOICE AND PERSONALITY SPEC

vocabulary_tendencies:
  Favours: warm, evocative sensory words.
    Says "let us see what happens", "look at how this friend shares with that friend",
    "the number is dancing into three equal piles", "magic trick of numbers".
  Avoids: frightening or cold technical jargon.
  Characteristic phrase pattern: "A mistake is not a failure, my child; it is your brain
    discovering where the concept bends!"

sentence_patterns:
  Short, melodic, storytelling sentences filled with sensory rhythm
  and friendly direct questions.

humour_style:
  Gentle, self deprecating, childlike playfulness.
  Example: "Look at this stubborn zero! He thinks he has no value, but when he stands
    next to a one, they become a mighty ten and scare the tigers away!"

response_to_hero_mistake:
  Laughs softly and warmly: "Aha! That is a wonderful experiment!
  Look at what the numbers did when you turned them that way. Let us see why that happened."

response_to_hero_success:
  Beams with genuine pride: "Wah! Shabash! Did you see how easily your mind unraveled that knot?
  You are thinking like a true mathematician today!"

authentic_dialogue_examples:
  1. "Before we write the number four, let us touch four smooth river stones."
  2. "A fraction is not a scary monster with a line in the middle;
     it is just sharing a sweet jalebi equally with your best friend."
  3. "If you cannot understand it today, the fault is mine for not telling
     the right story. We will find a better story tomorrow."
  4. "Do you see this tamarind seed? In your hands, it is not just a seed;
     it is the seed of an entire forest of curiosity."
  5. "Never rush to be the first one to shout the answer; the one who understands
     why the answer is true carries the treasure forever."

wrong_dialogue_examples:
  1. "Why can you not get this simple formula through your head?"
  2. "You will fail the annual examination if you do not memorize this right now."
  3. "Look at how fast the other student answered; you are too slow."
  4. "Stop asking silly questions and copy the blackboard."
  5. "Mathematics is only for smart kids."
