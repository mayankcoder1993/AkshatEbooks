full_name: Dr. Meera Iyer

series: SGK-SCI

domain: Applied physics, cryogenic propulsion, planetary mechanics,
  energy transformations, scientific instrumentation

age_at_series_inception: 44

professional_role:
  Experimental Physicist and former Propulsion Systems Specialist at ISRO.
  Contributed to cryogenic stage testing for heavy satellite launch vehicles.
  Currently heads an advanced experimental laboratory exploring renewable energy
  storage and mentors young researchers and STEM students.

formative_experience:
  what_happened: SEALED until planned reveal.
    [Internal note: During a critical ground qualification test of an indigenous
    cryogenic upper stage engine, an unexpected pressure fluctuation triggered
    an automatic abort two seconds before ignition. The telemetry software flagged
    sensor malfunction. Meera went into the sub zero test bay, traced the physical
    liquid hydrogen feedline, and discovered thermal acoustic resonance caused by
    a valve geometry vibration that the computer simulation had dismissed. She proved
    that physical reality always outranks simulated assumptions.]
  when: Six years before series inception, during launcher trials.
  consequence: She is obsessed with tangible physical verification. Her teaching
    forbids calculating mathematical equations until the student has physically
    observed, felt, or constructed the phenomenon with real materials.
  reveal_plan: Book 2 of the SGK Science series, Mission 3.

core_belief:
  The universe obeys inviolable physical laws; our mathematical equations
  are merely polite attempts to describe them. If the physical experiment
  contradicts your equation, the equation is incomplete.

teaching_philosophy:
  Physical phenomenon first, intuitive mechanical model second, formal mathematical
  derivation third. Demands that learners predict experimental outcomes before
  releasing the pendulum or measuring the voltage.

principled_refusal:
  Will never teach physics through formula memorization without mechanical intuition.
  Any student who solves an exam numerical by blindly substituting variables into an
  unexplained formula without sketching the free body diagram is sent back to the workbench.

## SECTION C: VOICE AND PERSONALITY SPEC

vocabulary_tendencies:
  Favours: kinetic and experimental verbs.
    Says "conservation of momentum", "entropy gradient", "phase transition",
    "resonance frequency", "empirical verification", "boundary condition".
  Avoids: passive textbook abstraction.
  Characteristic phrase pattern: "Do not guess; measure the temperature change and let nature answer."

sentence_patterns:
  Fast paced, energetic, punctuated with sharp rhetorical questions
  that force learners to observe physical indicators.

humour_style:
  Playful, energetic scientific humor teasing mathematical arrogance.
  Example: "The equation claims this rocket will fly to Mars, but gravity
    did not read your spreadsheet."

response_to_hero_mistake:
  "Look at your ammeter needle. It is swinging backward.
  Nature is telling you something about your polarity; listen to her!"

response_to_hero_success:
  "Look at that interference pattern! That is the wave nature of light,
  revealing itself right on your desk! Now write down the wavelength equation."

authentic_dialogue_examples:
  1. "Before you write a single integral, close your eyes and feel where
     the kinetic energy is flowing."
  2. "A rocket nozzle is not metal; it is a thermodynamic throat transforming
     chaotic molecular heat into pure directional momentum."
  3. "Do not fear an anomalous data point; the greatest discoveries in science
     began not with 'Eureka!', but with 'That is strange...'"
  4. "Cryogenic temperatures do not forgive careless seals. Precision is your
     only survival gear."
  5. "You computed the answer in three decimal places, but your units are kilograms
     instead of Newtons. You just equated mass with force!"

wrong_dialogue_examples:
  1. "Just memorize the three laws of motion for the upcoming quiz."
  2. "Experiments take too much time; let us stick to textbook proofs."
  3. "Physics is only for geniuses; ordinary students will never understand."
  4. "It does not matter why the bulb lights up, as long as you pass."
  5. "Computer simulations are always superior to hands on testing."
