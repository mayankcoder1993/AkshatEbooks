## SECTION A: CORE IDENTITY

full_name: Justice Balakrishnan Iyer

series: SGK-LAW

domain: Constitutional law, statutory interpretation, writs,
  fundamental rights, civil liberties, administrative jurisprudence

age_at_series_inception: 68

professional_role:
  Retired Supreme Court Justice and Senior Constitutional Scholar.
  Thirty five years on the bench across High Courts and the Supreme
  Court of India. Author of landmark judgments on privacy, administrative
  fairness, and federal balance. Currently trains senior advocates
  and civil service aspirants because he believes the Constitution
  lives only when ordinary citizens comprehend its safeguards.

formative_experience:
  what_happened: SEALED until planned reveal.
    [Internal note: During the 1975 Emergency, as a young advocate in
    the Madras High Court, he drafted a habeas corpus petition for an
    unlawfully detained journalist. Senior counsel advised him to drop
    the matter to avoid professional retaliation. Balakrishnan refused,
    argued the petition before a courageous single bench, and secured
    interim release hours before the judge was transferred. The experience
    taught him that statutory text without moral courage is mere paper.]
  when: 1975, early in his legal practice.
  consequence: His teaching philosophy insists that every legal doctrine
    must be examined from both sides: petitioner liberty versus state order.
    He never allows students to quote precedent without articulating the
    human consequence beneath the ratio decidendi.
  reveal_plan: Book 2 of the SGK Law series, Mission 3, during a high
    stakes constitutional crisis moot scenario.

core_belief:
  Statute is ink on paper; constitutional morality is the heartbeat
  within it. A lawyer who knows only the letter of the law is a mechanic;
  a jurist understands why the guarantee was forged in history.

teaching_philosophy:
  Employs a strict two sided adversarial method. Forces the student to
  argue the petitioner case with passionate rigor, then immediately
  compels them to defend the state action using equal statutory force.
  Only when both positions are exhaustively articulated does he reveal
  the balanced constitutional holding.

principled_refusal:
  Will never validate an answer that quotes case law without explaining
  the factual dispute and the legal ratio decidendi. Rote citation of
  AIR or Supreme Court Cases volume numbers without conceptual reasoning
  is dismissed with quiet firmness.

## SECTION C: VOICE AND PERSONALITY SPEC

vocabulary_tendencies:
  Favours: jurisdictional precision.
    Says "locus standi", "ultra vires", "manifest arbitrariness",
    "proportionality test", "doctrine of severability".
  Avoids: emotional rhetoric without statutory foundation.
  Characteristic phrase pattern: "Counsel, examine the proviso.
    What does the legislature withhold in clause three?"

sentence_patterns:
  Measured, periodic sentences with classical balance.
  Begins with the constitutional premise, qualifies with the limitation,
  then concludes with the question of rights.

humour_style:
  Gentle, judicial wit highlighting statutory paradoxes.
  Example: "The legislature drafted twenty pages of exemptions,
    leaving the citizen with the undisputed right to breathe,
    provided permission is sought in triplicate."

response_to_hero_mistake:
  Listens without interrupting. Pauses.
  Asks: "If your argument prevails, counsel, what prevents the executive
  from arresting your client tomorrow under the same logic?"

response_to_hero_success:
  "The Court notes your submission with approval. That is the ratio.
  Now defend the counter position with equal vigor."

authentic_dialogue_examples:
  1. "The Constitution does not exist to protect popular speech;
     it exists precisely because unpopular speech requires a shield."
  2. "You have cited Kesavananda Bharati. Excellent. Now tell me
     which of the eleven opinions forms the true binding majority on this point."
  3. "Do not read the Fundamental Right in isolation; Article fourteen,
     nineteen, and twenty one form a golden triangle that breathes together."
  4. "The State may claim public emergency, but procedure established
     by law cannot be suspended by administrative convenience."
  5. "Stand straight, counsel. You are not begging a favor;
     you are asserting a constitutional guarantee."

wrong_dialogue_examples:
  1. "Just memorize Section 302 and pass the exam."
  2. "The government can do whatever it wants in practical life."
  3. "Let us skip the legal philosophy and look at the answer key."
  4. "You did a super job today, buddy!"
  5. "The judge was obviously biased, so the ruling is worthless."
