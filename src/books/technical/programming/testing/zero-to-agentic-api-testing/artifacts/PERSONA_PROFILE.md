## Primary Buyer (the person who clicks Buy Now)

Arun Pillai, 27, QA analyst, Chennai. 3.5 years manual testing. Employed, no formal programming education, basic HTML and spreadsheet formulas only. Reads on a laptop at a desk after dinner, phone on commute for reviews only.

Time constraint: 1 to 1.5 hours on weeknights, about 2 hours on weekends. Realistically 10 to 12 hours per week.

Knowledge state:

  Genuinely knows: manual test case design, bug reporting, regression checklists, what a URL and a JSON body look like.

  Thinks he knows but does not: "APIs work like websites", "200 means the feature works", "automation means recording my clicks".

  Afraid to admit: he cannot read JavaScript, has never opened a terminal with intent, and does not know what "the wire" is.

Urgency level: HIGH. CI/CD migration timeline plus a scheduled interview.

Learning style under pressure: Worked examples he can retype, then a checklist to verify himself. Not summaries. Not flashcards. He wants to SEE the thing run.

Success definition: "Success means running the book's Newman suite from a terminal with zero failures and explaining any one assertion's failure message without looking it up."

Failure fear: Being exposed in the automation reorganization as the person who still clicks, three years into his career.

Desired identity: The person on the team who owns the quality gate. The one developers ask "did the suite pass?" before merging.

Emotional hurdles (per ap04 and vertical v01): fear of the command line, fear of JavaScript syntax, memorization fatigue from documentation style reading, imposter feelings around engineers.

Transformation arc: Hiding avoidance (Ch 1) to first guided win (Ch 2 to 3) to overconfident copying (Ch 4) to humbled by his own silent false positive (Ch 5) to methodical rebuilding (Ch 6 to 8) to owning the gate under pressure (Ch 9 to 13).

## Reading Mode Consequences for This Book

Chapter independent: NO. Chapters are sequential by design; the World Bible Chapter Opening Condition enforces continuity.

Daily plan: YES. FORMAT_DECISION specifies a 6 to 8 week schedule; the frontmatter Reader's Roadmap shows a fast path (4 weeks, 2 chapters per week) and a full path (8 weeks, 1 chapter plus practice per sitting).

Physical context: Desk with laptop. Workbench screens are therefore sized for side by side reading: selectable text, minimum 10 pt in HTML, no screenshot of code the reader could copy.

Vocabulary: Full professional vocabulary for testing; JavaScript explained once then used freely; network concepts explained with physical analogies (restaurant waiter, hotel keycard).

Sentence length: Dialogue lines maximum 25 words; prose paragraphs maximum 50 words.

Gameplay intensity: MEDIUM. Scenario challenges yes, timed drills only for the interview prep callouts, no competitive framing.
